Accreditation is one path of many.
What can Pursuit begin in the near term, and what should it prepare for over the long term?

Workforce Pell and new rules for accreditors could open federal funding to programs such as Pursuit's, though only through accredited institutions. This brief offers a dual approach: funded partnerships that Pursuit can begin in the near term, and low-cost preparation that keeps an accredited Pursuit college available as a long-term option. Outcomes evidence connects the two.

02The landscape

How a new college gets approved

A new college passes through four steps in order, each with a different gatekeeper. Federal aid can begin at candidacy, but only after the Department of Education approves participation. Credit transfer is a separate decision made by receiving universities.

  1. Step 1

    State authorization

    Requires a New York Board of Regents charter, and each degree program must be registered. Existing colleges in the region are canvassed for objections.

    Gatekeeper: NYSED
  2. Step 2

    Candidacy

    An accreditor reviews eligibility, including enrolled students, a governing board and audited finances. Nonprofits in candidacy can become eligible for federal aid.

    Gatekeeper: Accreditor
  3. Step 3

    Accreditation

    Typically granted four to five years after candidacy. Recent precedents took five to ten years from founding.

    Gatekeeper: Accreditor
  4. Step 4

    Federal aid

    Requires a participation agreement with the Department of Education. Workforce Pell programs also need approval from the governor and the Department.

    Gatekeeper: Department of Education
  5. If applicable

    Transfer

    Each receiving university decides which credits it accepts. This sits outside the accreditor's control.

    Gatekeeper: Receiving universities

Recent developments relevant to the decision

Money follows earnings
70 / 70completion / placement

Workforce Pell covers programs of 150–599 hours that run 8 to under 15 weeks. Programs qualify only if 70% of students complete, 70% find work, and the program passes a value-added earnings test. Awards are prorated by program length.

Outcomes data becomes the basis for funding. Pursuit's 7-month program is too long for Workforce Pell and fits regular Pell instead.

New accreditors can enter
2new accreditors seeking federal recognition

The Department of Education reports that only one institutional accreditor has launched in the past 40 years. Its new accreditation rule from the Accreditation, Innovation, and Modernization (AIM) rulemaking, finalized Oct 30 and effective July 1, 2027, lowers barriers for new accreditors. Two new accreditors are seeking federal recognition: PSC, an outcomes-based accreditor, and CPHE, formed by public university systems in the South.

The change widens access to federal aid. Transfer and employer recognition remain with receiving universities and employers.

Peers are becoming colleges

Acquiring over building, so far

Organizations such as Campus, Outsmart, Frontier and Stepful have acquired small accredited schools to gain their state licenses and accreditation. Merit America is an uncommon example of a builder: Meritus College, founded in 2025, is registered in Utah to grant degrees and is not accredited by a federally recognized accreditor.

An acquisition brings a license and the seller's accreditor. Each new program still needs approval.

Entry-level jobs are shrinking
−13%ages 22–25, most AI-exposed jobs

Stanford payroll research finds early-career workers in the most AI-exposed occupations lost 13% relative employment since late 2022, while older workers in the same jobs held steady. The authors' explanation is that AI substitutes for codified, book-learned knowledge more than for tacit knowledge built on the job.

If that holds, supervised work gains value relative to classroom credentials. The New York Fed cautions the slowdown can't be pinned on AI alone.

Earn-and-learn gets funded
$300M+federal apprenticeship funding, 2026

The Labor Department announced over $300M in apprenticeship funding this year and awarded Pursuit $4.7M in October. North Carolina lets community colleges award up to 16 credit hours for work-based learning in apprenticeship-aligned associate (AAS) programs.

Employers cover apprentice wages, and federal grants fund intermediaries like Pursuit. Neither requires Pursuit's own accreditation.

On transfer. Each receiving school decides which credits it accepts, whatever the accreditor. The Government Accountability Office (GAO) found transfer students lost 43% of their credits on average, and 94% when moving from for-profit to public schools. The Accreditation, Innovation, and Modernization (AIM) rulemaking committee agreed colleges should disclose what will transfer, explain denials and offer an appeal.

04Proposed plan

Revenue and credentials, connected by outcomes evidence

Working hypothesis

Funded partnerships can grow enrollment and revenue in the near term. Low-cost preparation in parallel keeps an accredited Pursuit college available as a long-term option.

Suggested near-term priorities
RevenueDecide who pays for new seats. Consider union funds, public programs and employers who already pay for training.
CredentialsCUNY or SUNY in the near term, and Pursuit's own over the long term.
Outcomes evidencePrioritize the earnings record that payers, colleges and accreditors review.

Illustrative sequence

Months 0–6

Begin funded partnerships

Each can begin without accreditation.

Upskilling for current workers

Union funds, public programs and employers already pay to train people in work. Union training funds pay members' tuition from collectively bargained money. Local workforce boards can spend up to 20% of federal adult and dislocated worker funds on incumbent worker training. Employers pay from learning budgets, with up to $5,250 per employee tax-free each year. Pursuit's pilot with Goldman Sachs is a starting point.

Within 3 months: price a standard module and identify who controls each fund.

College credit for the AI-Native program

CUNY faculty can approve credit for the program under Policy 1.21. Graduates could leave with CUNY credit toward a degree, which addresses the degree requirement some face later in their careers. The faculty review also maps Pursuit's skills to credit, and an apprenticeship, a Workforce Pell module or a later accreditation review could reuse that mapping. Pursuit has been a registered apprenticeship sponsor since 2023. In an expanded model, Pursuit can deliver the related instruction, an employer pays the apprentice, and a CUNY or SUNY college awards credit for the instruction.

Within 6 months: submit the curriculum for faculty review and explore apprenticeship terms with one employer.

Workforce Pell module with LaGuardia

An 8 to 14 week program offered by LaGuardia, with Pursuit delivering up to 25% under contract (under 50% with accreditor approval), could test federal aid without Pursuit owning a college.

Within 6 months: confirm LaGuardia's interest and the state approval timeline.

Months 0–12, in parallel

Invest in the foundations for accreditation

Low-cost work that also strengthens the near-term partnerships.

Seat-level economics

What one learner and one placement cost Pursuit in each program, against what each payer provides per seat. The comparison shows which funding sources cover their costs.

Outcomes evidence

Measure employment and earnings for every entrant, including those who leave early, with independent verification. Prepare a study plan for the AI-Native model so that a randomized or comparable evaluation can start once the program is stable.

Credential groundwork

Define credit by demonstrated skills, such as building and deploying an AI application, so that curriculum updates need no new approval. Scope the Board of Regents charter, a separately chartered entity, and the standards of outcomes-based accreditors such as PSC.

From month 12, as conditions are met

Decide whether to pursue a Pursuit college

The case for a college is strong when these conditions hold.

  • Evidence. A randomized trial or comparable study has been commissioned and quantifies the earnings effect, and placement has held at target across several cohorts.
  • Demand and funding. Union funds, public programs and employers pay for enough seats that tuition has identified payers, and funders have committed to the setup years.
  • Partner limits. College approval timelines or the federal limit on outside delivery constrain growth, and Pursuit seeks a credential in its own name.
  • Accreditor fit. An outcomes-based accreditor is federally recognized, has accredited a new institution, and accommodates a curriculum that changes frequently.

06Tradeoffs

Tradeoffs between options

Margin vs. mission

Upskilling pays more per seat but serves people who already hold jobs, and it competes with the core program for staff time.

Funded seats vs. discounted seats

A member discount is quick to arrange, but learners still pay and few enroll. A seat paid in full by a fund or employer fills, but the commitment takes longer to negotiate.

Learner value vs. Pursuit revenue

An apprenticeship with college credit gives learners the most: a wage, a state credential and credit. It pays Pursuit the least, because wages, tax credits and state instruction funds go to employers and colleges. Upskilling pays Pursuit directly and carries no credit.

Fast growth vs. exposure

Union and public channels can fill many seats quickly, but they tie revenue to one fund or one administration and draw regulatory scrutiny. Eastern Gateway Community College grew rapidly through a union free-college program, lost access to Pell funds in 2022 and closed in 2024.

Rigor vs. program change

A randomized trial gives the strongest proof but needs a stable program and a control group that goes unserved. The AI-Native program changes every few months.

A review of randomized evaluations by Katz, Roth, Hendra and Schaberg finds that sectoral programs raise earnings mainly by moving participants into higher-wage jobs in the targeted sector. Gains were largest at mature providers with established employer relationships.

With a WorkRise grant, Pursuit and J-PAL-affiliated researchers designed a randomized trial of the earlier fellowship, as described in Pursuit's 2025 report.

Read with care. The college-based Year Up changed several things at once, so the comparison can't isolate who teaches. The pattern still points one way: the gains travel with design, coaching and employer ties. Owning a college keeps those, unless accreditor rules on inputs such as faculty credentials push the model toward the college version.

New York and CUNY specifics
  • CUNY credit for prior learning (Policy 1.21). Faculty can approve credit for organization-specific training. Once recorded, the credit transfers across CUNY on the same basis as regular credit, and equivalencies post in CUNY's transfer database.
  • Reconnect. Free tuition, fees and books in high-demand associate programs, including AI and technology, for New Yorkers aged 25–55. Degree holders qualify from January 2027. It pays after federal and state aid, and requires at least 6 credits per semester.
  • Workforce Pell in New York. The state portal opened Aug 24, 2026. The institution must have operated in its current structure for 12 months, and a nonprofit can only deliver part of a program under contract with an eligible college.
  • State authorization. No entity may confer degrees or use the words college or university in New York without a charter. Out-of-state colleges need accreditation before operating in person or online in New York.
